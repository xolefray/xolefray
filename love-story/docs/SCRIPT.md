## Recording tips

Speak so each line fits its window. A line that runs past its end collides with the next picture. The gaps between windows are pauses; leave them. The film ducks the music under the spoken line and brings it back in the gap.

Record one take per language, from the top of the film, and export it as mp3, m4a, or wav. The file starts at film time 0:00, with silence until the first line, so the words land on these times when the film plays. Name them `audio/narration.en.mp3` (or `.m4a` or `.wav`) and `audio/narration.es.mp3`. How the film finds the file is in the [README](../README.md).

## Retiming

If a take runs long, change the numbers in [`src/timeline.js`](../src/timeline.js), not the words.

- Widen that line's `end` in `NARRATION`. If it would overlap the next line, move that next line's `start` (and its `end`) by the same amount.
- The picture is timed to `BEATS`. Move the beat that belongs to the long line, and any later beats the line now runs into, so the pop-up still matches the voice.
- If the line crosses a page turn, move that row in `LEAF_TURNS` and the matching `start`, `open`, `close`, and `end` on `SPREADS`. Spread k opens while leaf k−1 turns; those two tables have to agree.
- If the whole film grows, raise `DURATION` and move `END_CARD` (`start`, `fadeStart`, `end`).

Captions, page titles, the lamp, the score, and the camera's turn shots read those exports. Cue times inside a spread follow `BEATS` only where that spread computed them from `BEATS`.
